🎯 Real talk — the one-paragraph truth

There's a world-class engine bolted to a bicycle. The expertise is elite — 27+ years HR, Forbes Coach Council, Brainz Magazine, and a client list most consultancies would kill for (ByteDance, Novartis, VISA, Mastercard, Heineken). But the digital presence is doing almost none of the selling. Three separate websites that don't link to each other, not one of them has a blog, and the social accounts are either dormant (Peatix: 1 follower), tiny (BHD LinkedIn: 22 followers), or invisible (Instagram/YouTube counts hidden, nothing posted at scale). Someone who Googles this brand today finds fragments, not a story — and certainly not proof worthy of the credentials.

The good news: this is the easiest kind of client to grow. The authority already exists — it just isn't packaged, distributed, or converting. That's exactly the gap a proper website + social build closes.

🔧 What needs to improve — per property

🐦 HummingBeing

"Beautiful niche, zero distribution. The most shareable product of the three — and the least marketed."

The problems

  • No social accounts at all — yet TRE "shaking" is perfect short-form video
  • No blog → invisible for "TRE Singapore", "somatic coaching" searches
  • Pricing fully hidden; only a WhatsApp/form CTA
  • Testimonials are anonymous one-liners — no faces, no names

The fixes

  • Launch Instagram Reels + TikTok: TRE demos, "what is somatic" explainers
  • Add a blog targeting local + somatic-wellness SEO keywords
  • Google Business Profile for "TRE / somatic Singapore" local search
  • Publish "from $X" pricing + named, photo testimonials
  • Email capture + a simple "intro to TRE" nurture sequence

🏢 BHD Asia

"The credibility flagship — but a 22-follower LinkedIn page badly undersells a firm that coaches ByteDance and VISA."

The problems

  • LinkedIn company page: 22 followers, typos in "specialties" (looks sloppy)
  • No case studies despite a killer client list
  • No Insights/blog hub; Forbes/Brainz articles live elsewhere
  • "Events" section but no content/news engine behind it

The fixes

  • Rebuild the LinkedIn company page + 3×/week cadence & employee advocacy
  • Turn client wins into (anonymised) case studies — proof sells B2B
  • Build an Insights hub; republish every Forbes/Brainz article here (own the authority)
  • Lead magnet: "Leadership Resilience" guide → email list
  • Fix the specialty typos immediately

🦉 Partage Consulting

"The thinnest of all — a near-empty storefront whose newsletter promises articles that don't exist."

The problems

  • Effectively a single page; /about, /services, /articles all 404
  • Newsletter sign-up with no published content to deliver
  • Zero social footprint on any platform
  • No testimonials, no case studies, no Japan-market localisation

The fixes

  • Actually publish the articles — back the newsletter promise
  • Fix the broken pages; build out real services/about pages
  • Localise EN + JP; tap Kyoto expat & ACCJ networks
  • Decide: keep separate, or fold into one brand to stop diluting effort

👤 Isabelle (personal brand)

"Your personal LinkedIn is the entire funnel — and it's running on 14–33 reactions a post. The Forbes/Brainz authority is your single biggest unused asset."

The problems

  • Engagement modest & inconsistent; lots of reshares vs original
  • YouTube, Instagram, Pinterest exist but barely used
  • Noomii stuck on free plan — bio & reviews hidden
  • No content repurposing pipeline

The fixes

  • Fixed posting cadence + 4 content pillars (TRE · leadership · resilience · founder story)
  • Repurpose each Forbes/Brainz piece → LinkedIn post → Reel → YouTube short
  • Launch a LinkedIn newsletter (owned audience, native reach)
  • Upgrade Noomii + actively collect written reviews
  • One link-hub bringing all 3 brands + booking together

🚀 The build plan — from website up to social media identity

How we'd take this client from "fragmented & underselling" to "one coherent brand that converts." Foundation first, social identity layered on top of a site that's actually ready to receive the traffic.

1
Phase 1 · Weeks 1–4 · Foundation

Brand architecture & website rebuild

Decide the structure (one umbrella brand, or a clean hub-and-spoke with the personal brand at the centre). Rebuild on a fast, SEO-ready stack with clear CTAs, transparent pricing, named testimonials, client logos, and analytics + pixels wired in from day one. Cross-link all properties.

2
Phase 2 · Weeks 3–8 · Content engine

Own the authority you've already earned

Stand up a blog/insights hub on each site. Republish every Forbes & Brainz article (with canonical tags). Build 2–3 lead magnets (resilience guide, TRE intro, leadership checklist) and an email automation that nurtures to a booking. This is what makes SEO and social actually convert.

3
Phase 3 · Weeks 5–12 · Social media identity

Build the brand handles & a real posting machine

Secure & design consistent brand profiles (LinkedIn company, Instagram, YouTube, TikTok). Create a visual identity kit + templates. Ship a content calendar driven by short-form video (TRE demos convert), founder thought-leadership, and client-proof. Turn the personal LinkedIn into the amplification hub feeding all three brands.

4
Phase 4 · Ongoing · Amplify & measure

Paid retargeting + monthly reporting

Layer in light paid retargeting once organic + content are live. Track the metrics that matter — followers, engagement rate, email list growth, qualified leads, bookings, cost-per-lead — in a monthly dashboard, and double down on what moves revenue.

✅ If we only did 5 things first

  • Cross-link the three sites & the personal profile — stop leaking authority between brands (1 day).
  • Republish the Forbes/Brainz articles onto owned blogs — instant content + SEO + credibility.
  • Fix BHD's LinkedIn page (typos, banner, cadence) — it's the highest-credibility, lowest-effort win.
  • Launch one short-form video channel (TRE on Reels/TikTok) — the product is inherently shareable.
  • Publish pricing + named testimonials — remove the two biggest conversion blockers.
